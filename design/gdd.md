## 1. Player Promise

> You are a champion of Antrom. You raid fortresses with a party for gear you keep, while every run takes three minutes.

The twist is the run length, and the first minute proves it. For the listing rather than the sentence: *co-op crawling in the spirit of Minecraft Dungeons, in runs short enough for a public world.*

---

## 2. First Minutes & How to Play

*The order of events is what the live build does; the timings come from watching newcomers at -17,123. Most players press Enter the hall in under a minute; the champion and outfit screens are where the slow ones linger.*

| Time | Player experience |
|---|---|
| **0–5 seconds after control** | A torchlit stone hall, a title card, six language flags and one gold button: New game (or Continue with your champion's name). |
| **5–10 seconds** | Four champions turn on a pedestal: Vanguard (sword), Berserker (axe), Scout (bow), Striker (staff), each with a one-line role. Click one. |
| **10–60 seconds** | Change body, hair and armor, or press Enter the hall. You appear in the Hall of Antrom, name over your head, others visible, a war table in the middle. |
| **1–3 minutes** | Step onto the war table: three realms, the first fortress open. Make a party or Go alone, Start run. Black-stone rooms and your first fight: E swing, F heavy, Space block, Ctrl roll. Coins pop from the first kill. |
| **3–10 minutes** | At level 2 a NEW SKILL card puts your first skill on key 1. A weapon drops with a rarity toast. The boss door, and the Warlord. |
| **Natural stopping point** | FORTRESS CLEARED: time, coins, XP, gear found; "The Vaults is open to you." In V0 the screen also shows your time against the week's board and the set piece still missing. Back in the hall, the wardrobe shows where it drops. |

**Player-facing How to Play**

- Pick a champion, gather a party, enter
- Slay the Warlord: gear drops, next fortress opens
- Friends fight beside you and can raise you

## 3. Core Loop

| # | Step (verb) | What the player does (Player input → what they see or hear → what changes) | Why do it again? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Gather** a party in the hall | Make a party or Join an open one from the list → seats fill, ready checks light up → the leader picks a fortress (7 today across 3 realms) and a difficulty (Normal / Hard / Nightmare). Or Go alone. | A different fortress, a harder difficulty, or a party that makes the deeper ones possible. |
| 2 | **Fight** room to room | WASD, E light / F heavy / Space block / Ctrl roll, skills on 1–4 → enemies stagger, take numbers, drop coins and hearts → rooms clear, the boss door opens. | Your class's four skills open at levels 2, 5, 9 and 14, so the same room plays differently a week later. |
| 3 | **Slay** the Warlord | Boss arena, three phases → THE PARTY HAS FALLEN or FORTRESS CLEARED → coins and any weapon or armor drop go to every hero in the party the moment it falls. | Each realm's armor set drops only in that realm, piece by piece; the boss is the best roll. |
| 4 — optional | **Equip** in the hall | Wardrobe: all equipment, class-locked sets shown with where each missing piece drops → equip changes the hero everyone sees. | The set is visible x/N; the next piece has a named place to go. |
| 5 — optional | **Descend** into the Pit | The hall's summoning circle → The Pit of Chains, up to 8 heroes against the Chained Colossus; downed heroes are raised by an ally standing beside them → raid-only armor sets. | The one fight that needs more than a party. |

| | |
|---|---|
| **One complete loop takes** | **2–3 minutes** for a run of The Dark Fortress on Normal. A run ends on FORTRESS CLEARED or THE PARTY HAS FALLEN; the summary shows time, coins, XP and gear; clearing opens the next fortress in the realm. Payoffs inside the run: coins and hearts per kill, XP per kill, a level-up card, a weapon toast. |
| **Decision, challenge, or expression** | Class and loadout; which fortress and which difficulty; when to block, roll and spend stamina on a skill; who takes the boss's attention while others hit; in the Pit, who raises whom. |
| **Shortest satisfying visit / typical session** | One run, **3 minutes** / four to six runs with a hall visit between them, **15–25 minutes** |
| **Why repetition 10 differs from repetition 1** | First, **the party**: four classes and up to four players, so a Berserker and a Scout clear a room differently from two Strikers, and a stranger in the fourth seat changes it again. Second, **the hunt**: each armor set's missing piece drops in one named fortress, so run ten is the run you are there for the shoulders. Third, **difficulty**: Hard and Nightmare multiply enemy health and damage (and coins ×2, ×3) in a fortress you already know. Layouts are fixed, so fresh play comes from people and loot, not new rooms. |

**Pillars**

1. **Better with a party, never blocked without one.** Solo is always allowed; a party makes the deeper fortresses possible.
2. **Gear you keep, that others can see.** Every boss can drop a piece you wear back into the hall.
3. **Combat with weight.** Block, roll, stagger, a heavy blow that lands.

## 4. Why Players Come Back

*First session is bought with fun, next-day with an appointment, long-term with friends. Decentraland sends no notifications, so the reason to return lives in the player's memory or in their friends.*

### 4.1 The next-day (D1) sentence

> "A player who enjoyed their first session returns the next day because **the weekly fastest-clear board** shows their name a few seconds behind someone else's, and the board **resets Monday 00:00 UTC**, so the time they beat it must be set this week."

### 4.2 The progression chain

> **Repeatable action → persistent reward → new capability, status, or social role → new decision or challenge**

| Moment | What persists or has been built? | What becomes possible next? | How can another player tell? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **End of first session** | A champion at level 2–3 with one skill, a dropped weapon, the first fortress cleared and the second open, a clear time on the board. | The Vaults; Hard difficulty on the fortress just cleared; a party, now that the player knows the controls. | The hero's name and time on the war-table board; the dropped weapon in hand in the hall. |
| **End of first week** | A champion around level 10 with three skills; one realm set at 3/5 with the missing pieces named; one piece tempered to +1 at the Forge Lord; a board time in the top ten on at least one fortress; two or three names they have run with more than once. | Hard on the whole first realm, the Fallen Crown open, the Pit reachable with a group; the choice each evening between chasing a board time, a missing piece, or Nightmare coins for the next temper. | The +1 trim on the armor and the set's realm colours in the hall; their name on the war-table board; their hero in someone's OPEN PARTIES list. |
| **Week 3+ — what takes more than two weeks?** | A complete Pit set tempered to +3. The raid-only pieces drop only from the Chained Colossus, which takes a group (cleared with four so far, room for eight), and +3 costs Nightmare coins. | Carrying newer players through the Pit; setting board times on Nightmare; the next realm's set from scratch. | The full Pit set with +3 trim on a hero standing in the hall: the piece a newcomer asks about. |

*End of first week as a scene:* You come into the hall on a Sunday and check the board first: your Vaults time is fourth, eleven seconds off a name you have started to recognise, and the board resets tomorrow. Two heroes you ran with on Thursday are in an open party for The Throne Room, where your missing Fallen Crown shoulders drop, so you join before they start. Three runs later you have the shoulders and enough Hard coins to temper them to +1 at the Forge Lord, and the trim shows when you turn to face the hall. On the way out you run The Vaults once more, alone, and take third.

*Currency:* coins drop from every kill and clear (×2 on Hard, ×3 on Nightmare). They are spent at the **Forge Lord** in the hall *(V0 build)*: **reforge** re-rolls a dropped piece's stat line, **temper** raises a set piece one tier (+1 / +2 / +3), each tier costing more and adding a visible trim to the armor. Coins leave the game on every use; there is no trade and no cash purchase. Main abuse risk: a strong party carrying a low-level hero through Nightmare for the Warlord's prize, which today is paid to every hero in the party; V0 pays the prize only to heroes who dealt damage in the boss arena. Kill coins are already picked up per hero.

### 4.3 Two return hooks

| Selected hook | Exact trigger or timing | What the player anticipates | Reminder channel + no-reminder fallback |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Collection — realm armor sets** *(live today)* | Every Warlord kill can drop a piece of the realm's class set; the wardrobe shows the set x/N and names the fortress each missing piece drops in. | "The Fallen Crown shoulders drop in The Throne Room; two more runs and the set is done." When complete the set is worn in the hall, and the Pit sets sit beyond it. | No reminder needed: the wardrobe shows the gap every visit; fallback is the set itself seen on another player in the hall. |
| **2. Weekly fastest-clear board** *(V0 build)* | Best clear time per fortress and difficulty, top 10 with hero names, on the war table and a board in the hall; resets **Monday 00:00 UTC**, automatic, no content work. | "I was 4 seconds off third. Tomorrow, with a Scout in the party." The player who is knocked off sees who did it. | Community channel posts Monday's winners; fallback is the board itself, and the player's own memory of a time that is beatable. |

*Not in V1, parked for later rounds: daily contracts, guilds (crew obligation), a season track — see §9 cuts and `design/ideas.md`.*

## 5. Social by Design

*Playable alone, better with others.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **The repeatable social loop** | A player opens a party at the war table and picks a fortress → a second player sees it under OPEN PARTIES with the fortress, difficulty and class seats, and joins → the leader can now start a difficulty the two of them could not solo, and each covers what the other's class lacks (a Scout keeps range while a Vanguard holds the Warlord) → the clear time goes on the board under both names and the drop lands for both, so the same two are worth finding again. |
| **The disappearance test** | Solo runs, the hall, the wardrobe and the board still work; what breaks is **the Pit** (the Colossus has been cleared by four, never by one; a lone hero is not raised when downed) and **Nightmare** on the later fortresses, which are tuned above what one class can cover. A solo player keeps roughly two thirds of the game and loses the top of it. |
| **From strangers to a group** | The OPEN PARTIES list names the fortress, the difficulty and the seats already taken by class icon, so a newcomer sees "Throne Room · Hard · Vanguard, Striker, — , —" and knows a Scout or Berserker is wanted before they click. Ready checks light up per seat; no text needed. In V0 the party seat also shows the hero's level, so nobody joins a Nightmare run at level 3 by accident. |
| **Recognition & continuity** | Names float over heroes in the hall and in the run, and the war-table board lists them by fortress; a player first learns a name when it sits above theirs on the board or when a party seat fills beside them. What persists: the weekly board (a rival's name to beat), set progress on the hero others can see, and OPEN PARTIES that show a familiar name. |
| **Quiet hours & player counts** | When few people are online, a solo player can run any open fortress alone ("Go alone") and use the hall, wardrobe and training yard. Social play becomes viable at 2 players (one party). The ideal group is **4**, one of each class; a party holds up to **4**, the Pit holds up to **8** (v1 tested maximum: **4**, a full party in a fortress and the Pit cleared with four). How a solo arrival meets someone: the hall shows who is IN THE HALL and lists OPEN PARTIES to join with one click; the Pit's raid party never disbands and anyone can drop in while the fight is up. |
| **Drop-in / drop-out** | A party is joined from the hall before Start run; once a run starts the party is closed. In the Pit anyone can drop in mid-fight, and a downed hero is raised by an ally standing beside them; if the whole Pit party falls, everyone is put back in the hall and the Colossus is chained whole again. If a party member leaves mid-run the others continue. |
| **Visible play (the bystander test)** | In the hall's training yard a bystander sees a hero block, roll behind a dummy and land a heavy blow that staggers it, and understands the fight is timed hits, not clicks. |
| **Shareable play (the memorable moment)** | Eight heroes on the lava rim as the Chained Colossus drops, the last of them raised from the floor two seconds before the killing blow, and the raid-set drop toast over everyone's screen at once. |
| **Bring-a-friend** | The moment a player opens the Fallen Crown on Hard and the party list is empty, or the summoning circle to the Pit lights and they know nobody has done it alone. What changes when the friend arrives: a difficulty that was a wall becomes a run, and the clear time carries both names. |

## 6. Mobile-First

*The live build is keyboard-only and has been tested on desktop. Touch controls are in the V1 four-week scope (§9), so the mapping below is what V1 builds, and the mobile check is a V1 deliverable rather than a promise.*

**Every core-loop verb on touch**

| Core-loop verb | How it works with touch controls |
|---|---|
| **Gather** a party | The war table and summoning circle are trigger areas, not hover targets; OPEN PARTIES is a full-screen list with thumb-height rows; Join and Start run are one tap each. |
| **Fight** room to room | Left thumb moves. One large attack button on the right: **tap = light blow, hold = heavy blow**. Block is a second button beside it; **roll is a third button** (the mobile client has no gesture support, so no swipes anywhere). Two skill buttons above attack (slots 1–2); slots 3–4 reached by a swap tap in V1. The top-down camera never needs a second thumb. |
| **Slay** the Warlord | Same buttons; phase changes are telegraphed by the boss's wind-up animation and a bar that spans the top of the screen, readable at arm's length. |
| **Equip** in the hall | Wardrobe as a one-column list with large slot cards; equip is a tap, no drag. |
| **Descend** into the Pit | Trigger area on the circle; the raise-an-ally action is the same attack button held while standing beside a downed hero. |

**UI plan.** During a run only the health and stamina bars, the skill buttons and the room count are on screen, all in the bottom third within thumb reach; everything else (party list, summary, wardrobe) is a full-screen panel that opens between rooms or in the hall.

**Performance.** Target: 60 fps on recommended desktop at 4 players (met today in KOA testing); 30 fps on a **Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 8** (the developer's phone; tested folded, at phone width, and unfolded) at 4 players. The known risk is **asset weight**: the deployed scene is about 186 MB across three Synty realm kits, and the mobile Explorer has not yet opened the scene. Plan: load only the hall and the current fortress's kit (models are already split into per-realm and per-purpose files, so nothing structural stands in the way), compress textures per the SDK7 mobile guidance (budget numbers to be looked up, not quoted from memory), and measure on the named phone before any mobile promise is made.

**Desktop-only dependencies.** Checked against the mobile missing-features list (reviewed August 2026). In the loop: keyboard input for combat and skills, which the touch layer replaces; the seam exists, since input is already read through one controls module (`combatControls`), so touch is a second input source behind the same actions, not a redesign. Around the loop: scene lights (`LightSource`) on torches and braziers ship on mobile in v1.13.0 (September 2026), and until then the emissive textures carry the look; the UI panel kit uses nine-slice backgrounds, which mobile stretches instead of tiling, so the V1 touch UI uses flat panels with a border texture. Nothing in the design depends on gestures, hover, voice chat or daily quests.

## 7. World, Look & Story

**Story / world**

Antrom's three strongholds fell to a Warlord, a turned garrison and a Forge Lord who never stopped working, and the Hall of Antrom is where its champions gather to take them back room by room. Each stronghold keeps its own armor, so what you wear says where you have been.

**Visual direction.**

Synty low-poly fantasy kits (Dark Fortress, Fantasy Kingdom, Dwarven) seen from a top-down crawler camera, so a room, its doors and its enemies fit one screen and stay readable on a phone; heroes and enemies carry name tags and health bars; objectives are lit (the boss door, the loot glow); UI is a gold-on-dark stone panel kit with serif headings. **Signature:** a party of four, one of each class, in a torchlit black-stone room seen from above, name tags and health bars over each, the boss door lit ahead: the typical moment, not the spectacular one. Sound: each class's hit has its own weight (axe thud, bow snap, staff crack); on a muted phone the stagger animation and the damage number carry the same information.

## 8. Audience & Comparables

**Audience.** For players who already enjoy co-op action games with loot, arriving alone at a public parcel and hoping to find someone to run with, looking for a fight they can finish in the minutes they have.

**Comparables** — *one outside Decentraland, one inside.*

| | Minecraft Dungeons (Mojang, 2020) | Wondermine (Decentraland, 2020–) |
|---|---|---|
| **What works** | Four friends, one screen's worth of dungeon, gear that defines your role instead of a class menu; a difficulty ladder that makes a cleared level worth a second visit. Proof that a low-poly co-op crawler needs no story to be played for months. | A loop that Decentraland players came back to daily for years: walk in, swing the pickaxe at meteors, bank the gems, craft a better pickaxe. Everyone stands in the same field, so the game was social without a party system. |
| **What does not fit** | Levels run 15–20 minutes and the party is formed before the level, so a friend who shows up late waits. The camp is private: nobody else ever sees the gear you found. Both assume everyone bought the same game on the same store. | The loop is a click on a timer, not a fight; nothing you do with your hands changes the outcome, so the skill ceiling is zero and a session is mostly waiting. The reward is a currency, not something worn. |
| **What we do differently** | Three-minute runs so a late friend catches the next one; the hall is shared, so the set you finished is on your back where strangers can see it; the game lives in a public world, so "come play" is a link, not a purchase. | Combat with weight: block, roll and a heavy blow decide the room, and a Berserker beside a Scout plays differently from two Strikers. The thing you grind for is armor that shows, not a balance. |

## 9. 4 Week Plan (v1 scope)

*Team: one developer at about 20 hours a week, 80 hours across the four weeks. The game is already live at `-17,123`, so V1 ships as updates to the live scene rather than a new World; week 6's "live" milestone is met from week 1 and the public repository is `github.com/mfmcmillian/DG`. Proposed **V0 scope**: weeks 1–2 of this table, the board and the Forge Lord on the live loop, playtested at the two-player threshold; V1 adds the touch layer and the mobile pass.*

| Week | What is playable / done |
|---|---|
| 1 — Prototype definition (20 h) | Rename to Dungeons of Antrom in the title card and listing; Warlord's prize paid only to heroes who hit the boss; hero level shown on party seats; **weekly fastest-clear board** on the server with a Monday 00:00 UTC reset and the clear screen showing rank and gap. Playable: a run ends with "4th this week, 11 s behind Korrin". |
| 2 — Core interaction + first-group test (20 h) | **Forge Lord**: reforge and temper +1/+2/+3 with the visible trim; coin prices set from the clear-yield arithmetic. Milestone playtest at the social threshold: two players, one party, the board and the forge both used in one session; watch one true newcomer for §2's timings. |
| 3 — Core systems refinement (20 h) | **Touch layer**: move stick, tap/hold attack, block and roll buttons, two skill buttons, trigger areas at the war table, wardrobe and circle; run UI moved to the bottom third. Desktop unchanged. Asset pass begins: strip unused models from the deploy (453 MB in the repo against ~186 MB shipped). |
| 4 — Playable prototype, final design direction (20 h) | **Mobile playtest** on the named phone: a first-time phone player clearing three rooms on touch and the 30 fps check at four players; fix what fails, load per-realm assets on demand if the frame rate demands it. Board, forge and touch all live at `-17,123`; design direction locked from the week-2 and week-4 sessions. |

**What keeps the experience changing after launch**

- Without building a new level, we can rotate **the board's featured fortress and difficulty** every **Monday** (the reset is automatic; the feature is one server value).
- If an update is skipped, **the Monday reset and the drop tables** still create variation: a fresh board and a set still unfinished.
- Progression never resets and creates only a modest gap; **sets, tempers and board history persist**; a new player can contribute within **3 minutes** through **a party seat on Normal, where a fresh hero still clears rooms and shares the Warlord's drop**.
- One player behaviour that would change what we build next: **players forming the same party three evenings running** would move guilds (crew obligation) to the front of V2.

**Not building in v1**

1. **Daily contracts.** The hurtful cut: a daily hook is the surest D1 lever, but it needs the coin sink to exist first. Comes back first in V2 if V1 hits its numbers.
2. **Guilds / crews.** The strongest long-term hook known, and too much surface (roster, shared goals, moderation) for 80 hours.
3. **New fortresses or realms.** Seven fortresses and the Pit are enough content for the numbers V1 is measured on; content is consumed once, hooks are not.

*Standing non-goal: never randomised fortress layouts sold as the reason to return, because the promise is people and loot, not new rooms.*

**Top risk + fallback.** The touch layer and the 186 MB scene meeting on a phone in week 4 with no earlier mobile evidence. Fallback: if the scene does not hold 30 fps on the named device at four players by mid-week 4, V1 ships touch controls with the hall and **the first realm only** loading on mobile, the other realms desktop-only until the asset pass finishes; the board, forge and party remain identical across both.
